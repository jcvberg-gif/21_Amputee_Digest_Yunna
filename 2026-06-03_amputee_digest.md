## 🔥 HIGH (3)

### 1 · A Step in the Right Direction: Osseointegration Satisfaction & Mobility in Lower-Limb Amputees
**PMID [41662687](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41662687/)** | Published Feb 9, 2026 | Bucket A03

**Study**: Cross-sectional validated prosthesis-experience survey of lower-limb amputees who received OI implants at a single center, administered 1 month–6 years post-op. Concurrent nerve/soft-tissue reconstruction (TMR/RPNI) performed by a plastic surgeon at OI implantation in a subset.

**Results**: Mean satisfaction 7.56/10. Daily step counts and stepping cadence increased significantly at 1-year post-OI versus pre-OI baseline. Concurrent nerve reconstruction improved comfort scores further. Residual complaints: wet-environment use, stairs, and uneven terrain.

**Pearl**: OI delivers durable satisfaction gains, but the functional ceiling is set by prosthetic component selection and gait rehab intensity — not the implant alone.

**Clinical takeaway**: For patients failing socket fit, OI + concurrent peripheral nerve reconstruction (TMR/RPNI at time of implantation) is now a viable single-stage pathway. Refer to an OI-capable center before the patient reaches chronic pain or persistent skin breakdown.

**Action tag**: 🔧 PRACTICE CHANGE — discuss OI pathway for refractory socket-fit failures

### 2 · TMR and RPNI: The Evolving Landscape in Postamputation Pain & Prosthetics
**PMID [41549863](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41549863/)** | *JAAOS* Feb 1, 2026 | Bucket A05

**Study**: JAAOS clinical review. Describes indications, surgical principles, perioperative technique, and postoperative management of TMR and RPNI for pain prevention and prosthetic control enhancement.

**Results**: Both techniques reroute transected nerve input to new muscle targets, preventing symptomatic neuroma. Evidence synthesis: statistically significant reductions in PLP and RLP pain scores and PROMIS domains vs. standard management. **Primary (prophylactic) TMR/RPNI significantly outperforms secondary (rescue) intervention** on both pain severity and pain interference.

**Pearl**: Timing is everything — retrofitting is harder than prevention. Primary intervention at or shortly after amputation is the dominant prognostic variable.

**Clinical takeaway**: Any patient scheduled for elective amputation (dysvascular, oncologic, trauma) should have TMR/RPNI discussed before the surgical date, not after chronic pain develops. Build this into your pre-op checklist.

**Action tag**: ⚡ REFERRAL TRIGGER — integrate TMR/RPNI into pre-amputation surgical planning

### 3 · From Amputation to Persistent Pain: Molecular & Cellular Processes in Phantom Limb Pain
**PMID [41828335](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41828335/)** | *Int J Mol Sci* 27(5):2107, March 2026 | Bucket A04

**Study**: Comprehensive narrative review synthesizing peripheral, spinal, and supraspinal molecular/cellular pathways driving PLP.

**Results**: Three-level cascade — (1) **Peripheral**: TRPV1 upregulation, pro-inflammatory cytokine release, ectopic discharge from neuromas; (2) **Spinal**: altered AMPA/NMDA receptor trafficking, microglia/astrocyte activation enabling central sensitization; (3) **Supraspinal/cortical**: maladaptive S1 remapping, reduced GABAergic inhibition. Adaptive vs. maladaptive neuroplasticity at each level determines chronicity. No single-level intervention sufficient for established PLP.

**Pearl**: PLP is a cascade, not a site. Rational therapy maps intervention to level: RPNI/TMR for peripheral driver; neuromodulation (SCS, TMS) for spinal; graded motor imagery/mirror therapy for cortical remapping.

**Clinical takeaway**: For refractory PLP, take a level-specific history (burning/neuralgic/lancinating = peripheral; constant aching/central sensitization features = spinal; postural/movement-responsive = cortical) to prioritize therapy.

**Action tag**: 🧠 PATHOPHYSIOLOGY UPDATE — rational framework for multimodal PLP treatment selection

## 📝 MEDIUM (6)

### 4 · Redefining Prosthetic Needs: Upper Limb Loss Systematic Review
**PMID [41600539](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41600539/)** | *Sensors* 26(2):734, Jan 22, 2026 | Bucket A02

Caldas A, Matos D, de Eyto A, Martins N. PRISMA SR; 31 studies (2016–Dec 2023). Despite continuous technological advances, device abandonment persists. Functional performance, **weight**, and **intuitive control** are inseparable from confidence, embodiment, social participation, and acceptance. Calls for biopsychosocial prosthetic prescription models.

**Pearl**: Weight and control intuitiveness are the top two modifiable abandonment drivers — check these explicitly at every upper-limb fitting review.

### 5 · TMR/RPNI Awareness and Pain Outcomes: A Nationwide Survey
**PMID [40967608](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40967608/)** | *J Reconstr Microsurg*, 2025 | Bucket A05

REDCap survey; n=294 amputees (1,377 contacted via Amputee Coalition). Only **13%** had primary TMR/RPNI; **7%** secondary. Primary TMR/RPNI: significantly lower pain severity (p=0.019) and pain interference (p=0.046). Most patients had never heard of these procedures before the survey.

**Pearl**: Most eligible amputees still don't know TMR/RPNI exists. Patient awareness is the implementation gap, not supply.

### 6 · Meaningful Outcome Domains in LMIC Prosthetic Rehab (Qualitative SR)
**[PMC12853489](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12853489/)** | *BMJ Open* Jan 28, 2026 | Bucket A11

Fewins CJ, Ostler C, Donovan-Hall M. Qualitative synthesis (ENTREQ guidelines); LMIC settings. Central themes: access to a **durable prosthesis enabling work** and daily living; social acceptance; independence. Durability and work participation dominated over aesthetics or high-performance gait outcomes. Implication: procurement for global health programs should prioritize robust socket systems over advanced microprocessor components.

**Pearl**: In LMICs, the "ideal outcome" is a prosthesis that survives the terrain and lets you keep your job — not a K4 carbon blade.

---

### 7 · Pain and Functional Outcomes After Lower Limb Amputation: Scoping Review
**PMID [41807005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41807005/)** | 2026 | Bucket A10

Searched MEDLINE, Embase, PsycINFO (inception–Aug 15, 2025). Heterogeneous assessment methods preclude quantitative synthesis. Pain (PLP, RLP, LBP) and functional outcomes are frequently measured in isolation; simultaneous measurement remains rare. Calls for standardized paired outcome sets in future LLA research.

**Pearl**: Publish LLA rehab outcomes with a standardized paired set — e.g., PLP/RLP NRS + 2MWT or K-level — to make data comparable with the literature.

### 8 · Physical Activity as a QoL Tool in Lower Limb Amputees: Scoping Review
**[PMC12841301](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12841301/)** | *Healthcare* 14(2):253, Jan 20, 2026 | Bucket A10

Delbello F, Zullo L, Giacomini A, Bizzarini E. 615 studies identified; 18 included (6 SRs, 5 RCTs, 4 case-control, 2 cross-sectional). Physical activity interventions consistently improved QoL and physical performance in LLA. Both structured exercise and incidental activity approaches showed benefit; sedentary post-amputation lifestyle is modifiable.

**Pearl**: Prescribe movement — structured or unstructured — as standard in every LLA discharge plan.

---

### 9 · Winning the Arms Race: Upper Extremity Amputation Optimization
**PMID [41637591](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41637591/)** | *Bull Hosp Jt Dis*, Dec 2025 | Bucket A02

Comprehensive update: externally powered vs. hybrid prostheses, pattern recognition control, UE osseointegration, and concurrent TMR/RPNI at index surgery. Transhumeral OI + TMR/RPNI at primary surgery now described as viable single-stage option at experienced centers.

**Pearl**: For transhumeral amputees, the question is no longer "can we do OI + TMR/RPNI together?" but "does this patient have access to a center that does it?"

### 10 · Functional Outcomes With Osseointegrated Prostheses in Transfemoral Amputations

Potter BK et al. Prospective; n=41 TF amputees, 37 at 24-month follow-up (Oct 2017–Oct 2023). Functional mobility and prosthetic wear time improved significantly by 24 months. Stoma infection most common complication; meticulous skin hygiene protocols required.

### 11 · Transhumeral Osseointegration: Patient-Reported Outcomes
**PMID [39982023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39982023/)** | 2025 | Bucket A03

Socket-related issues alleviated in most post-transhumeral OI patients. PRO scores improved across QoL and function domains. Careful patient selection required given transdermal implant risks (infection, periprosthetic fracture); higher-demand UE users benefit most.

### 12 · Functional Outcome Measures for Pediatric Upper Limb Deficiencies (SR)
**PMID [40498878](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40498878/)** | *Prosthet Orthot Int*, Jun 2025 | Bucket A09

PRISMA SR; 19 reports; 4 performance-based tests + 6 PROMs evaluated. No single ideal measure exists. **ACMC** (Assessment of Capacity for Myoelectric Control) and **AHA** (Assisting Hand Assessment) are most evidence-supported for pediatric UL deficiency. Highlights persistent measurement gap.

## 🌍 International Rehab Spotlight

### Item 1 · IRF PM&R Fellowship Expanding in Sub-Saharan Africa
The **International Rehabilitation Forum (IRF)** runs 2-year PM&R physician fellowship programs in **Ghana, Ethiopia, and Cameroon**, with fellows rotating through the largest prosthetics and orthotics center in West Africa. Entry: practicing physicians in sub-Saharan Africa. The 8-week boot camp covers core PM&R principles; specialty blocks include orthotics/prosthetics, neurology, and orthopedics. Active recruitment as of early 2026.

*Why it matters*: This is the African PM&R specialist pipeline — building the workforce that makes prosthetic rehabilitation sustainable at scale, directly aligned with WHO Rehabilitation 2030 workforce targets.

→ [rehabforum.org/africa-fellows](https://rehabforum.org/africa-fellows/)

## 🌏 Korea & Travel pick of the week (Rotation 3 — UNESCO Cultural Heritage)

### Gangneung Danoje Festival · June 15–22, 2026

**What**: One of Korea's oldest living festivals (~1,000 years). UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity since 2005. Held annually in **Gangneung, Gangwon Province** on the East Sea coast.

**Why go**: 71 programs across 8 days — shamanistic rituals, Gwanno mask drama (non-verbal, uniquely Korean, hilarious), Odokddegi folk music, oral narrative poetry, and Korea's largest temporary outdoor marketplace (*Nanjang*). Attended mainly by Koreans — not touristy.

**Signature experiences**:
- Wash your hair in iris-water (*changpo*) — the festival's most iconic ritual, said to ward off bad luck
- Gwanno Mask Dance: watch and participate; no Korean required
- Nanjang market: regional street food, handicrafts, contests, and circus performances (budget a full afternoon)
- Side trip to **Anmok Coffee Street** (10 min by car): high-quality Korean pour-over with East Sea backdrop

**Getting there**: KTX Cheongnyangni → Gangneung, ~2h30. Book hotels now — the June 15–22 window fills fast.

Sources: [Korea.net](https://www.korea.net/Events/Festivals/view?articleId=8238) | [UNESCO ICH](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/gangneung-danoje-festival-00114)
