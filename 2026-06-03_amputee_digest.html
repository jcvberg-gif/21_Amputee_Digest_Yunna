🔥 HIGH (3)

1 · A Step in the Right Direction: Osseointegration Satisfaction & Mobility in Lower-Limb Amputees HIGH

PMID 41662687 | Published Feb 9, 2026 | Bucket A03

Study: Cross-sectional validated prosthesis-experience survey of lower-limb amputees who received OI implants at a single center, administered 1 month–6 years post-op. Concurrent nerve/soft-tissue reconstruction (TMR/RPNI) performed in a subset at time of OI implantation.

Results: Mean satisfaction 7.56/10. Daily step counts and stepping cadence increased significantly at 1-year post-OI vs. pre-OI baseline. Concurrent nerve reconstruction improved comfort scores further. Residual complaints: wet-environment use, stairs, uneven terrain.

💎 OI delivers durable satisfaction gains, but the functional ceiling is set by prosthetic component selection and gait rehab intensity — not the implant alone.

Clinical takeaway: For patients failing socket fit, OI + concurrent peripheral nerve reconstruction (TMR/RPNI at time of implantation) is now a viable single-stage pathway. Refer to an OI-capable center before the patient reaches chronic pain or persistent skin breakdown.

🔧 PRACTICE CHANGE — discuss OI pathway for refractory socket-fit failures

2 · TMR and RPNI: The Evolving Landscape in Postamputation Pain & Prosthetics HIGH

PMID 41549863 | JAAOS Feb 1, 2026 | Bucket A05

Study: JAAOS clinical review. Indications, surgical principles, perioperative technique, and postoperative management of TMR and RPNI for pain prevention and prosthetic control enhancement.

Results: Both techniques reroute transected nerve input to new muscle targets, preventing symptomatic neuroma. Statistically significant reductions in PLP and RLP pain scores and PROMIS domains vs. standard management. Primary (prophylactic) TMR/RPNI significantly outperforms secondary (rescue) intervention on pain severity and interference.

💎 Timing is everything — retrofitting is harder than prevention. Primary intervention at or shortly after amputation is the dominant prognostic variable.

Clinical takeaway: Any patient scheduled for elective amputation (dysvascular, oncologic, trauma) should have TMR/RPNI discussed before the surgical date. Build this into your pre-op checklist.

⚡ REFERRAL TRIGGER — integrate TMR/RPNI into pre-amputation surgical planning

3 · From Amputation to Persistent Pain: Molecular & Cellular Processes in Phantom Limb Pain HIGH

PMID 41828335 | Int J Mol Sci 27(5):2107, March 2026 | Bucket A04

Study: Comprehensive narrative review synthesizing peripheral, spinal, and supraspinal molecular/cellular pathways driving PLP.

Results: Three-level cascade — (1) Peripheral: TRPV1 upregulation, pro-inflammatory cytokine release, ectopic neuroma discharge; (2) Spinal: altered AMPA/NMDA trafficking, microglia/astrocyte activation enabling central sensitization; (3) Cortical: maladaptive S1 remapping, reduced GABAergic inhibition. No single-level intervention sufficient for established PLP.

💎 PLP is a cascade, not a site. Rational therapy maps to level: RPNI/TMR → peripheral; neuromodulation (SCS, TMS) → spinal; graded motor imagery/mirror therapy → cortical remapping.

Clinical takeaway: For refractory PLP, take a level-specific history (burning/neuralgic = peripheral; constant aching/central sensitization = spinal; postural/movement-responsive = cortical) to prioritize therapy.

🧠 PATHOPHYSIOLOGY UPDATE — rational framework for multimodal PLP treatment selection

📝 MEDIUM (6)

4 · Redefining Prosthetic Needs: Upper Limb Loss Systematic Review MEDIUM

PMID 41600539 | Sensors 26(2):734, Jan 22, 2026 | Bucket A02

Caldas A et al. PRISMA SR; 31 studies (2016–Dec 2023). Device abandonment persists despite advances. Weight and intuitive control are inseparable from confidence, embodiment, and social participation. Calls for biopsychosocial prescription models.

💎 Weight and control intuitiveness are the top two modifiable abandonment drivers — check these explicitly at every upper-limb fitting review.

5 · TMR/RPNI Awareness and Pain Outcomes: A Nationwide Survey MEDIUM

PMID 40967608 | J Reconstr Microsurg, 2025 | Bucket A05

REDCap survey; n=294 amputees. Only 13% had primary TMR/RPNI; 7% secondary. Primary TMR/RPNI: significantly lower pain severity (p=0.019) and interference (p=0.046). Most patients had never heard of these procedures.

💎 Most eligible amputees still don't know TMR/RPNI exists. Patient awareness is the implementation gap, not supply.

6 · Meaningful Outcome Domains in LMIC Prosthetic Rehab (Qualitative SR) MEDIUM

PMC12853489 | BMJ Open Jan 28, 2026 | Bucket A11

Fewins CJ, Ostler C, Donovan-Hall M. ENTREQ qualitative synthesis; LMIC settings. Central themes: access to a durable prosthesis enabling work; social acceptance; independence. Durability and work participation dominated over aesthetic or high-function outcomes.

💎 In LMICs, the "ideal outcome" is a prosthesis that survives the terrain and lets you keep your job — not a K4 carbon blade.

7 · Pain and Functional Outcomes After Lower Limb Amputation: Scoping Review MEDIUM

PMID 41807005 | 2026 | Bucket A10

Searched MEDLINE, Embase, PsycINFO (inception–Aug 15, 2025). Pain (PLP, RLP, LBP) and functional outcomes frequently measured in isolation; heterogeneity precludes synthesis. Calls for standardized paired outcome sets in LLA research.

💎 Publish LLA rehab outcomes with a standardized paired set (e.g., PLP/RLP NRS + 2MWT or K-level) to make data comparable.

8 · Physical Activity as a QoL Tool in Lower Limb Amputees: Scoping Review MEDIUM

PMC12841301 | Healthcare 14(2):253, Jan 20, 2026 | Bucket A10

Delbello F et al. 615 screened; 18 included (6 SRs, 5 RCTs, 4 case-control, 2 cross-sectional). Physical activity consistently improved QoL and physical performance in LLA; both structured exercise and incidental activity approaches show benefit.

💎 Prescribe movement — structured or unstructured — as standard in every LLA discharge plan.

9 · Winning the Arms Race: Upper Extremity Amputation Optimization MEDIUM

PMID 41637591 | Bull Hosp Jt Dis, Dec 2025 | Bucket A02

Comprehensive update: externally powered vs. hybrid prostheses, pattern recognition control, UE osseointegration, concurrent TMR/RPNI at index surgery. Transhumeral OI + TMR/RPNI at primary surgery is now viable at experienced centers.

💎 For transhumeral amputees, the question is no longer "can we do OI + TMR/RPNI together?" but "does this patient have access to a center that does it?"

📋 Skim / LOW (3)

10 · Functional Outcomes With Osseointegrated Prostheses in Transfemoral Amputations LOW

PMID 39898879 | Clin Orthop Relat Res, Mar 2025 | Bucket A03

Potter BK et al. Prospective; n=41, 37 at 24-month follow-up. Functional mobility and prosthetic wear time improved significantly. Stoma infection most common complication; meticulous skin hygiene protocols required.

11 · Transhumeral Osseointegration: Patient-Reported Outcomes LOW

PMID 39982023 | 2025 | Bucket A03

Socket-related issues alleviated in most post-transhumeral OI patients. PRO scores improved across QoL and function. Transdermal implant risks require careful patient selection; higher-demand UE users benefit most.

12 · Functional Outcome Measures for Pediatric Upper Limb Deficiencies (SR) LOW

PMID 40498878 | Prosthet Orthot Int, Jun 2025 | Bucket A09

PRISMA SR; 19 reports; 4 performance-based tests + 6 PROMs evaluated. No single ideal measure exists. ACMC and AHA are most evidence-supported for pediatric UL deficiency.

🌍 International Rehab Spotlight

Item 1 · IRF PM&R Fellowship Expanding in Sub-Saharan Africa

The International Rehabilitation Forum (IRF) runs 2-year PM&R physician fellowship programs in Ghana, Ethiopia, and Cameroon, with fellows rotating through the largest prosthetics and orthotics center in West Africa. Entry requirement: practicing physician in sub-Saharan Africa. The 8-week boot camp covers core PM&R principles; monthly specialty blocks include orthotics/prosthetics, neurology, and orthopedics. Active recruitment as of early 2026.

Why it matters: This is the African PM&R specialist pipeline — building the workforce that makes prosthetic rehabilitation sustainable at scale, directly aligned with WHO Rehabilitation 2030 workforce targets.

→ rehabforum.org/africa-fellows

Item 2 · ISPRM Vancouver 2026 — Just Wrapped

The 20th World Congress of ISPRM (Vancouver Convention Centre, May 17–21, 2026) concluded with strengthened WHO, World Rehabilitation Alliance, and UN partnerships, building on ISPRM 2025 Marrakesh ("Global Solutions in Rehabilitation: (Re)visiting Africa Before 2030"). The back-to-back Africa → Americas arc reflects a deliberate global-equity agenda toward WHO Rehabilitation 2030 milestones.

→ isprm.org/events/isprm2026

Item 3 · What LMIC Amputees Actually Want (BMJ Open, Jan 2026)

The Fewins et al. qualitative SR (PMC12853489) across LMICs confirms patients consistently prioritize durability, work participation, and independence over gait aesthetics or K4 performance. The single most-cited barrier remains access to a prosthesis that survives daily use — directly actionable for WHO/OPEC Fund procurement decisions.

🌏 Korea & Travel pick of the week (Rotation 3 — UNESCO Cultural Heritage)

Gangneung Danoje Festival · June 15–22, 2026 🎭

What: One of Korea's oldest living festivals (~1,000 years). UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity since 2005. Held annually in Gangneung, Gangwon Province on the East Sea coast — right now is the perfect planning window.

Why go: 71 programs across 8 days — shamanistic rituals, Gwanno mask drama (non-verbal, uniquely Korean, hilarious), Odokddegi folk music, oral narrative poetry, and Korea's largest temporary outdoor marketplace (Nanjang). Attended mainly by Koreans — not touristy.

Signature experiences:

  • Wash your hair in iris-water (changpo) — the festival's most iconic ritual, said to ward off bad luck
  • Gwanno Mask Dance: watch and participate; no Korean required
  • Nanjang market: regional street food, handicrafts, contests, and circus performances (budget a full afternoon)
  • Side trip to Anmok Coffee Street (10 min by car): high-quality Korean pour-over with East Sea backdrop

Getting there: KTX Cheongnyangni → Gangneung, ~2h30. Book hotels now — the June 15–22 window fills fast.
