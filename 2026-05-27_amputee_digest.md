## 🔥 HIGH (7)

### 1 · Prosthetist-Led Comorbidity Screening RCT — A New Care Model (A10)

**Study** — RCT (BMJ Open, Feb 27, 2026; NCT05410548), University of Delaware lead. Adults with lower-limb amputation randomized to prosthetist-administered comorbidity screening (PHQ-9 for depression, suicidal ideation query, low back pain, peripheral vascular assessment, protective-sensation screen) vs usual care during routine prosthetic follow-up.

**Results** — Statistically significant improvement in comorbidity detection rates vs usual care. High acceptability by prosthetists and patients. Depression, neuropathy, and vascular disease identified and referred in a meaningful proportion of participants. Amputee comorbidity burden substantially exceeds general population baseline.

**Pearl** — The prosthetist visit is often more frequent than any physician visit in this population. This RCT proves that window can be exploited for holistic screening. A prosthetist asking "How's your mood?" is now evidence-based practice.

**Clinical takeaway** — Add a brief structured screen (PHQ-9 + protective sensation + pedal pulse check) to every PM&R–prosthetics encounter checklist. Model this for your residents and prosthetist colleagues.

**Action tag**: 🔧 Protocol change

[PMID 41760154](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41760154/)

### 2 · TMR/RPNI — Prophylactic Pain Prevention in 45–100% of Patients (A05)

**Study** — Comprehensive evidence review (PubMed 2025) synthesizing TMR and RPNI outcomes across multiple cohorts and centers for postamputation neuroma pain, phantom limb pain, and prosthetic control quality.

**Results** — Neuroma pain: improved in **75–100%** of patients. PLP: improved in **45–80%**. Mean NRS reduction: **2.4–6.2 points**. Prophylactic use (at time of amputation): neuroma-pain absent in **48–100%**, PLP absent in **45–87%** at follow-up. Prophylaxis substantially outperforms salvage approaches.

**Pearl** — The optimal window for nerve management is the operating room at the time of primary amputation. Every PM&R consult on a pre-op or early post-op amputee is a window to influence surgical planning.

**Clinical takeaway** — At first PM&R contact for an amputee (pre- or post-op), document whether TMR/RPNI was performed and, if not, initiate surgical consultation for salvage consideration.

**Action tag**: 🔪 Surgical referral

[PMID 41549863](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41549863/)

### 3 · Transhumeral OI + TMR: Combined Surgical Approach Outperforms Socket Controls (A02 / A05)

**Study** — Preliminary observational cohort (PM&R, May 2025), n=3 transhumeral amputees with combined osseointegration (OI) + targeted muscle reinnervation (TMR) using myoelectric prostheses, compared to matched controls (socket prostheses, no TMR).

**Results** — Higher Brief Activity Measure for Upper Limb Amputation (BAM-ULA) scores vs controls; longer daily prosthetic use hours; improved intuitive myoelectric signal quality from TMR + elimination of socket pistoning/skin breakdown from OI. FDA investigational device exemption stage for upper-limb OI.

**Pearl** — OI + TMR creates a synergistic "double upgrade" for transhumeral amputees — mechanical anchoring eliminates fit issues while TMR provides neural intelligence to the myoelectric interface. This combination may represent state-of-the-art care for this underserved population.

**Clinical takeaway** — Transhumeral amputees presenting to PM&R clinic should have early multidisciplinary planning for combined OI + TMR feasibility assessment. Don't wait for socket problems to accumulate.

**Action tag**: 🔁 Multidisciplinary pathway

[PMID 40439344](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40439344/)

### 4 · PROCESS-Guided Primary TMR/RPNI Case Series: Prevention at the Source (A05)

**Study** — Case series (PROCESS reporting framework, PubMed 2025) documenting primary TMR and RPNI performed concurrently at time of amputation across multiple amputation levels and etiologies, for prevention of post-amputation and phantom limb pain.

**Results** — Prophylactic nerve management at primary amputation is feasible across trauma, dysvascular, and oncologic etiologies. Cases demonstrate early return to prosthetic rehabilitation without significant residual limb pain burden. PROCESS framework enables standardized reporting toward future RCT-quality evidence.

**Pearl** — The most important time to prevent phantom pain is before it starts. Pre-amputation PM&R consultation is not just about rehab goals — it's about influencing the surgical plan.

**Clinical takeaway** — When elective or anticipated amputation is planned (DM/PAD, oncologic), contact the surgical team and request concurrent TMR/RPNI consultation before the patient reaches the OR.

**Action tag**: 🔪 Surgical planning

[PMID 40992116](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40992116/)

### 5 · Diabetes Doubles 5-Year Mortality After Major Amputation — Welsh Cohort (n=2,542) (A06)

**Study** — Population-based retrospective cohort (PubMed 2025), n=2,542 individuals undergoing major lower-limb amputation in Wales. Primary outcome: impact of diabetes on short- and long-term mortality.

**Results** — **5-year mortality: 67.0% with diabetes vs 57.1% without** (HR 1.62, 95%CI 1.44–1.82, p<0.001). Paradox: diabetes associated with *reduced* 30-day mortality (likely reflecting hospital-level diabetes management). Long-term follow-up gap is the critical vulnerability. 2MWT and functional outcomes not primary endpoints in this cohort study.

**Pearl** — The early post-discharge period looks deceptively good for diabetic amputees — then the 5-year gap catches up. Intensive PM&R follow-up at 6-month intervals may be the intervention point that matters.

**Clinical takeaway** — At inpatient discharge of a diabetic amputee, schedule PM&R follow-up at 3, 6, and 12 months explicitly. Flag high-risk in EMR. Don't rely on PCP capture.

**Action tag**: ⚠️ Risk stratification

[PMID 40216200](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40216200/)

### 6 · What Counts as Recovery? LMIC Prosthetic Rehab Outcome Domains (Qualitative SR) (A11)

**Study** — Qualitative systematic review (BMJ Open, Jan 28, 2026; DOI 10.1136/bmjopen-2025-109817). ENTREQ-compliant, CINAHL/PsycInfo/Web of Science/Trip databases through April 2024. Lead authors: Fewins, Ostler, Donovan-Hall.

**Results** — Central outcome domain: **access to a durable prosthesis enabling work and daily living activities**. Secondary: social acceptance, independence, reintegration. Western outcome frameworks (K-level, 6MWT) systematically miss highest-priority LMIC patient domains. Calls for LMIC-specific outcome measure development. First systematic effort to define recovery from the LMIC patient's perspective.

**Pearl** — K3 walking is not the goal in Kampala. Employment is. This SR is the conceptual foundation for future LMIC-appropriate outcome instrument development. Cite it liberally in global health grant applications.

**Clinical takeaway** — If consulting on international prosthetic programs or global health projects, advocate for endpoints beyond mobility: work participation, community roles, household function.

**Action tag**: 🌍 Global lens

[PMC12853489](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12853489/)

### 7 · GHOST EEG-BCI + VR for Phantom Limb Pain: Pilot Neurofeedback Trial (A04)

**Study** — Single-center exploratory pilot neurofeedback trial (Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, Apr 7, 2026; DOI 10.3389/fnhum.2026.1697837), n=7 patients with chronic upper-limb PLP (amputation or brachial plexus avulsion, NRS ≥3/10). Intervention: GHOST portable EEG-based brain–computer interface + immersive VR, enabling patients to control a virtual limb via real-time motor imagery. 10 sessions over 2 weeks.

**Results** — Safety and feasibility confirmed. Short-term analgesic effects demonstrated, particularly for **paroxysmal pain** episodes. Supports sensorimotor re-engagement hypothesis: real-time feedback targets maladaptive cortical reorganization underlying PLP. n=7; confirmatory controlled trials needed.

**Pearl** — This device doesn't just show a virtual limb — it lets the patient *control* it with their motor cortex. That's a mechanistically distinct step from passive mirror therapy and may be more durable.

**Clinical takeaway** — For upper-limb amputees with chronic refractory PLP unresponsive to pharmacology + mirror therapy, BCI-VR programs are emerging as a complementary non-pharmacologic option. Watch this space for trial enrollment opportunities.

**Action tag**: 🔬 Emerging technology

[PMC13095819](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13095819/)

Comparative review (JOSR, Jan 26, 2026; DOI 10.1186/s13018-026-06675-w) of OPRA, ILP, OPL, and ITAP osseointegration systems — biomechanics, surgical technique, patient selection, outcomes. ### 9 · Microprocessor Knee Provision: Immediate PRO Gains Within 4 Weeks (n=29) (A10)

Prospective multi-center study (Apr 2025, n=29 TFA, 2 UK centers). Outcomes: ABC scale, RNLI, PEQ, 2MWT before and 4 weeks after MPK provision. Significant improvements (p<0.05) in all PROs + 2MWT. ABC score ↔ 2MWT r=0.62 (p<0.05). Physical activity (step count) unchanged in short term — confidence improves before behavior follows. **Pearl**: Don't wait for community step count improvement to confirm MPK benefit; PROs at 4 weeks are the early signal. [PMID 40202401](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40202401/)

### 11 · Growth and Prosthetic Provision in Pediatric Amputees: Cambodia (A09 / A11)

Mixed-methods study (PMC 2025) — prosthetist perspectives + clinical casefile analysis, Cambodia. Physical growth drives progressive socket fit failure and prosthetic length discrepancy; LMIC supply chains cannot support frequent replacement. Proactive growth-monitoring protocol needed. **Pearl**: Pediatric amputees in LMIC settings age out of prostheses faster than they can access replacements — a silent crisis in the global rehab landscape. [PMC12572713](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12572713/)

### Item 2 — International Rehabilitation Forum: PM&R Fellowship in Sub-Saharan Africa

The **International Rehabilitation Forum (IRF)** has established PM&R physician fellowship programs in **Ghana, Ethiopia, and Cameroon**, all LMIC contexts in sub-Saharan Africa. The model delivers a global PM&R curriculum with lifelong international mentorship, pairing LMIC-based trainees with high-income country PM&R specialists for virtual and in-person teaching. The goal: create self-sustaining, locally led rehabilitation medicine programs that address the critical physiatrist shortage across the continent — and directly expand prosthetic rehabilitation capacity.

📌 *Yunna note*: IRF actively recruits international mentors. PM&R fellowship-trained physicians with interest in global health are exactly the profile sought for virtual teaching rotations. Consider reaching out: [rehabforum.org/africa-fellows](https://rehabforum.org/africa-fellows/)
